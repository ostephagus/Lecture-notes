For this chapter we will consider a Newtonian, incompressible fluid with velocity:
\begin{equation*}
    \vec{u} = (u, 0, 0)
\end{equation*}
Note that incompressibility requires $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 0$ and so $u = u(y, z, t)$. This gives $(\vec{u} \cdot \nabla)\vec{u} = 0$. Therefore, \eqnref{eqnNavierStokes} becomes:
\begin{align}
    \rho \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} &= f_x - \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \mu \nabla_\perp^2 u \label{eqnNSOneDPll} \\
    f_\perp &= \nabla_\perp p \label{eqnNSOneDPerp}
\end{align}
Here the $\perp$ indicates the components perpendicular to the flow direction, along $y$ and $z$ axes.

If $\vec{f} = \vec0$, then $\frac{\partial p}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = 0$ and therefore we can say $-\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = G(t)$.
\section{Couette Flow}
Consider flow between two plates. The bottom plate is fixed and the top plate is driven at speed $V$. We see that $\mu \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}} = 0$. Including the boundary conditions $u(0) = 0$, $u(h) = V$,
\begin{equation*}
    u(y) = \frac{V}{h}y.
\end{equation*}
The stress and strain tensors are given by:
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & \gamma & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \implies e =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & \frac\gamma2 & 0 \\
        \frac\gamma2 & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \implies \sigma =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        -p & \mu \gamma & 0 \\
        \mu \gamma & -p & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & -p
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
Therefore, the stress exerted by the bottom plate on the fluid is given by $\sigma \vec{n}$ where $\vec{n} = -\vec{e_y}$: $\sigma\vec{n} = (-\mu \gamma, 0, 0)^T$. We can understand this as the fluid trying to pull the fluid along with it, so it must resist that with a force along $-x$.

Here the local dissipation rate is $\Phi = \mu \gamma^2$.
\section{Plane Poiseuille Flow}
Consider flow between fixed plates at $y = \pm h$. Let the pressure on the left be $p_+$ and on the right be $p_-$. Let the flow be steady. We have the equation:
\begin{equation*}
    \mu \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}} = \frac{\partial p}{\partial x}
\end{equation*}
and solving (using the boundary condition $u(\pm h) = 0$),
\begin{equation*}
    u = -\frac1{2\mu} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} \left(h^2 - y^2\right)
\end{equation*}
We can calculate flux per unit length in the $z$ direction:
\begin{equation*}
    q = \int_{-h}^{h} u~dy = -\frac{2h^3}{3\mu} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x}
\end{equation*}
and the vorticity:
\begin{equation*}
    \vec{\omega} = \nabla \times \vec{u} = \left(-\frac1\mu \frac{\partial p}{\partial x} y\right)
\end{equation*}
Therefore, we have a linear vorticity gradient with positive vorticity at $y = h$ and negative at $y = -h$.

The strain rate is:
\begin{equation*}
    e =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & \frac12 \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} & 0 \\
        \frac12 \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
    \implies \Phi(y) = \mu \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)^2
\end{equation*}
\section{Circular Poiseuille Flow}
Consider a circular pipe. Let $z$ be the flow direction and the pipe extend from $z = 0$ to $z = L$. Let the radius be $a$.

Suppose the pressure at $z = 0$ is $p_0 + \delta p$, and at $z = L$ it is $p_0$. Assume that the pipe behaves as a rigid boundary. 

Note that in the case of flow along a pipe between two large tanks of fluid, the non-parallel flow extends only a length of order $a$ into the pipe. Therefore, the above is a good approximation for this case provided $L \gg a$.

In cylindrical polar coordinates $(r, \theta, z)$,
\begin{align*}
    \frac{\partial p}{\partial r} &= \frac1r \frac{\partial p}{\partial \theta} \\
    0 = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} &+ \frac{\mu}{r} \frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(r \frac{\partial w}{\partial r}\right)
\end{align*}
where $\vec{u} = (0, 0, w)$. Furthermore, incompressibility requires:
\begin{equation*}
    \nabla \cdot \vec{u} = 0 \implies \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0 \implies -\frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = G \text{ const.}
\end{equation*}
Rearranging:
\begin{align*}
    &\frac1r \frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(r \frac{\partial w}{\partial r}\right) = -\frac{G}{\mu} \\
    &\implies \frac{\partial w}{\partial r} = -\frac{Gr}{2\mu} + \underbrace{\frac{A}{r}}_{\substack{0 \text{ for}\\\text{regularity}}} \\
    &\implies w(r) = \frac{G}{4\mu} \left(a^2 - r^2\right) \text{ using the no-slip condition.}
\end{align*}
Now we can find the volume flux:
\begin{align*}
    q &= \int_{0}^{2\pi} \int_{0}^{a} w~r~dr~d\theta  \\
    &= 2\pi \frac{G}{4\mu} \left[\frac{a^2 r^2}{2} - \frac{r^4}{4}\right]_0^a \\
    &= \frac{\pi G a^4}{8\mu}
\end{align*}
Our rate of strain tensor looks like:
\begin{equation*}
    e = 
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & 0 & \frac12 \frac{\partial w}{\partial r} \\
        0 & 0 & 0 \\
        \frac12 \frac{\partial w}{\partial r} & 0 & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
Then the stress is given by:
\begin{equation*}
    \sigma_{rz} = 2\mu e_{rz} = \mu \frac{\partial w}{\partial r} = -\frac{Gr}{2}
\end{equation*}
We can show that the total shear stress by the fluid on the tube is equal to the pressure force applied to the fluid, $\Delta p~\pi a^2 \vec{e_z}$. Furthermore, the total dissipation is equal to the rate of doing work -- this is found on Ex1Q5.

We want:
\begin{equation*}
    \mu \nabla^2 w = \frac{\partial p}{\partial z} = -G
\end{equation*}
with $w = 0$ on $x = \pm a, y = \pm b$.

We use IB Methods to solve this using a particular solution/complementary functio method, $w = w_p(x, y) + w_c(x, y)$. We consider, from Plane Poiseille flow,
\begin{equation*}
    w_p(x, y) = \frac{G}{2\mu} \left(b^2 - y^2\right)
\end{equation*}
which satisfies $\nabla^2 w_p = - \frac{G}{\mu}$. We therefore need $\nabla^2 w_c = 0$ along with the new boundary condition:
\begin{equation*}
    w_c = - \frac{G}{2\mu} (b^2 - y^2) \text{ on } x = \pm a.
\end{equation*}
After separation of variables, we obtain:
\begin{equation*}
    w_c(x, y) = \frac{Gb^2}{2\mu} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{4(-1)^n}{\lambda_n^3} \cos\left(\frac{\lambda_n y}{b}\right) \frac{\cosh\left(\frac{\lambda_n x}{b}\right)}{\cosh\left(\frac{\lambda_n a}{b}\right)}
\end{equation*}
where $\lambda_n = \left(n + \frac12\right)\pi$. Now we have this, the flux is given by:
\begin{align*}
    Q &= \int_{-b}^{b} \int_{-a}^{a} w(x, y)~dx~dy \\
    &= \frac{4 \Delta p}{3\mu L} ab^3 \left(1 - \frac{6b}{a} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{\tanh(\lambda_n a / b)}{\lambda_n^5}\right)
\end{align*}
This sum can be understood to be the  due to the effects of the sides.

We consider unidirectional flow $\vec{u} = (u(y, t), 0, 0)^T$ where $u(0, t) = U$ for $t > 0$. This must satisfy:
\begin{equation*}
    \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \nu \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}}
\end{equation*}
We have seen in IB Fluid Dynamics that the answer is a similarity solution, using the length scale given by the equations $\sqrt{\nu t}$. Note that the other possible length scales involve $u$ which is the flow along $x$, not along $y$. Recalling IB Fluid Dynamics, the solution is:
\begin{equation*}
    u(y, t) = U \operatorname{ercf}(\eta)
\end{equation*}
where $\eta$ is the similarity variable, $\eta = \frac{y}{2\sqrt{\nu t}}$. The complementary error function is:
\begin{equation*}
    \operatorname{ercf}(\eta) = \frac{2}{\pi} \int_{\eta}^{\infty} e^{-s^2} ds 
\end{equation*}
The stress by the fluid on the plate is $\mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$ and is given by:
\begin{equation*}
    \tau = -\frac{\mu U}{\sqrt{\pi \nu t}}
\end{equation*}
The rate of working by the plate on the fluid is $\vec{u} \cdot (\sigma \vec{n})$:
\begin{equation*}
    P = U\left(-\mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right) = \frac{\mu U^2}{\sqrt{\pi \nu t}}
\end{equation*}
This must equal the rate of change of kinetic energy plus the rate of dissipation. The easiest check for this is to integrate:
\begin{align*}
    \rho u \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} &= \rho u \nu \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}} \\
    \implies \frac{d}{dt} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac12 \rho u^2 dy &= \mu \int_{0}^{\infty} u \frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial y^{2}} dy \\
    &= \left[\mu u \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right]_0^\infty - \mu \int_{0}^{\infty} \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right)^2 dy 
\end{align*}
\section{Oscillating Flat Plate}
Consider a similar setup but now the plate moves with velocity $U\cos(\omega t)$. Consider a limit $t \to \infty$ so that the fluid undergoes steady oscillations. The solution (see IB Fluid Dynamics) is
\begin{align*}
    u &= \Re\left(U e^{i\omega t} e^{-\sqrt{\frac{i\omega}{\nu}}}\right) \\
    &= Ue^{-ky} \cos(\omega t - k y)
\end{align*}
where $k = \sqrt{\frac{\omega}{2\nu}}$.

Then the diffusion length in this case is $\frac1k$.

The time dependence of the stress complicates calculations like rate of working and dissipation. However, we are often interested in the time-averaged quantities. The quantities in the energy equation are quadratic, so we are interested in products of oscillatory functions:
\begin{align*}
    \avg{\cos^2(t)} = \avg{\sin^2(t)} &= \frac12 & \avg{\cos(t)\sin(t)} &= 0 \\
    \avg{\Re(Ae^{i\omega t})\Re(Be^{i\omega t})} &= \frac12 \Re(AB^*)
\end{align*}

We want \eqnref{eqnNavierStokes} in cylindrical polar coordinates:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial \vec{u}}{\partial t} - \frac{v^2}{r} \vec{e_r} = -\nabla p + \mu \frac1r \frac{\partial}{\partial r} \left(rv\right) \vec{e_z}
    \label{eqnNavierStokesCylinder}
\end{equation}
The individual components are:
\begin{align*}
    -\rho \frac{v^2}{r} &= -\frac{\partial p}{\partial r} \\
    0 &= \underbrace{-\frac1r \frac{\partial p}{\partial \theta}}_{\substack{0 \text{ by}\\\text{symmetry}}} + \mu \left(\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial r^{2}} + \frac1r \frac{\partial v}{\partial r} - \frac{v}{r^2}\right)
\end{align*}
and in these coordinates the strain rate tensor is:
\begin{equation*}
    e = \frac12
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 & \frac{\partial v}{\partial r} - \frac{v}{r} & 0 \\
        \frac{\partial v}{\partial r} - \frac{v}{r} & 0 & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 0
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}
and so the shear rate is:
\begin{equation*}
    \sigma_{r\theta} = 2\mu e_{r\theta} = \mu r \frac{\partial }{\partial r} \left(\frac{v}{r}\right).
\end{equation*}
